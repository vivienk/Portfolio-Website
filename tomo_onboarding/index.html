The challenge

Onboarding drop-off was high, and conversion was slow.

Users moved from landing page to email capture and pricing, then into a long application. That disconnected path obscured the “no credit score required” model and made sensitive requests feel unexplained.

Fragmented flow
Pricing appeared before a guided product entry.
Trust deficit
Users questioned account permissions and credit-check logic.
Hidden effort
One long, visually inconsistent form gave no sense of progress.

Solution overview

Make each step feel necessary, safe, and finishable.

The redesign joined education, verification, and activation into one progressive onboarding sequence.

Define / framing

The problem was uncertainty, not just form length.

The old paths split traffic before activation and asked for sensitive details without enough context. I framed the redesign around three decisions:

01 · Connect

One path into the product

Bring web and app entry into the same guided sequence, instead of asking people to decide on pricing before they understood the product.

02 · Explain

Earn trust at each request

Place the reason for name, identity, address, and phone data beside the field that asks for it.

03 · Guide

Make the finish line visible

Show progress, immediate feedback, and a clear next action through verification and activation.

Design decision analysis

Progressive disclosure made trust tangible.

01

Five guided KYC steps

Five screens did not add tasks; they made the existing KYC work easier to understand. Each step grouped related inputs, explained why they were needed, gave immediate feedback, and showed a direct next action.

02

Trust layer

Used partner logos, FDIC security badges, humanized illustration, and contextual copy to explain sensitive moments.

03

Resumable progress

Partnered with engineering on real-time validation and session persistence so people could continue where they left off.

04

Lifecycle extension

Added SMS engagement around verification to reconnect people with an unfinished application and support the goal of improving daily conversion.

Deliverables

A functional onboarding system, not a set of screens.

Copy decision · time and trust

In internal user testing, people spent an average of 1.75 minutes in onboarding. I led the first form screen with “less than 3 minutes” and security assurances, so the effort and purpose were clear before users entered personal data. “Low effort” meant a short, predictable path—not fewer required verification steps.

Takeaway · what I learned

More screens can feel like less work.

The goal was not to remove required KYC questions. It was to give each screen one clear job, explain sensitive requests at the moment they appeared, and show progress and feedback. I learned to judge onboarding effort by what users have to understand and decide—not by screen count alone.
